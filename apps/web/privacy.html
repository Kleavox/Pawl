{{SITE_NAME}} keeps what it needs to sign you in and keep your account safe: your email address, a username, and a proof of your password that cannot be turned back into it. 1. Who runs this machine

{{SITE_NAME}} at {{ROOT_HOST}}, including the sign-in pages at {{PASS_HOST}}, is run by the operator of {{ROOT_HOST}} ("we", "us"). We decide what is collected and why. Write to {{CONTACT_EMAIL}} with any question or request about your data.

2. What your account holds

  • Your email address and whether you have verified it.
  • Your username, and your role (an ordinary visitor or the owner).
  • The ways you sign in: a password, and, if you linked them, the account identifier Google or GitHub gives us for you.
  • When the account was created and when you last signed in.
  • A password verifier. Your password never reaches our servers: your browser derives a one-way verifier from it, and only that verifier is stored. We cannot read or recover your password.
  • An encryption key pair. Its private half is locked in your browser with a key derived from your password before it is stored, so we hold it but cannot open it.

3. Signing in with Google or GitHub

If you choose Google, we ask only for the scopes openid email profile: an identifier for your Google account, your email address and whether Google has verified it, and your name, which we read but do not keep. If you choose GitHub, we ask for read:user user:email: your public profile and your email addresses, of which we keep only the account identifier and one verified address. We use these only to create your account or sign you in to it. We do not post anything, read anything else, or keep the access Google or GitHub gives us beyond finishing the sign-in. This information is not sold and is never used for advertising.

4. Sessions and security records

  • A session for each device you sign in on: your browser's user agent, the IP address it was created from, and when it started. You see all three under Account → Devices. A session and what it holds last 7 days, or until you sign out or revoke it there.
  • Sign-in and account events (for example "signed in", "password changed") with your browser's user agent and a salted hash of your IP address; these records keep the hash, not the address.
  • Rate-limit counters keyed by a hash, which expire within an hour, so that nobody can guess passwords or codes at speed.
  • Codes we email you are stored as hashes for 10 minutes; email-verification links last 30 minutes and password-reset links 15 minutes, also stored only as hashes.
  • A human check by Cloudflare Turnstile before you create an account or reset a password.

5. Apps you sign in to with {{SITE_NAME}}

When an app asks to sign you in with {{SITE_NAME}}, it receives only what you allow on the consent screen: an identifier for you (a different one for each outside app, so apps cannot match you between them) and, with the matching scopes, your verified email address and your username. Apps run by the operator of {{ROOT_HOST}} also learn your role. Every app you have allowed is listed under Account → Apps, where you can disconnect it. Sign-in codes handed to an app last 60 seconds, the tokens it receives last 10 minutes, and the refresh tokens an outside app may hold last 30 days. Each app's own privacy policy covers what it does with what it receives.

Your account and everything tied to it stays until you delete the account. Sessions, codes, links and counters expire on their own as described above. Security events are kept to protect the service; when an account is deleted they stay without the link to it.
